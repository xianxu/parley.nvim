🤖:[ToolOpus*]

### Yes — two cameras, two different jobs

| Role | Device | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Main / imaging | R5 | Collects the actual photons for the picture |
| Guide | small mono cam (e.g. ASI120MM Mini) + 30–50mm guide scope | Watches one star at 1–3 s cadence, measures drift, tells the mount to nudge |

The guide camera never contributes to your image. It's a closed-loop sensor for the mount.

### What guiding actually corrects

- **RA periodic error** — the worm gear isn't perfectly round, so tracking oscillates over its ~8 min worm period. This is the dominant unguided failure mode.
- **Polar alignment residual** — even a good ASIAIR align leaves a few arcminutes, which becomes slow DEC drift.
- **Flexure / differential sag** in the camera–mount train.

It does **not** fix wind, footfalls on your roof, seeing, or an unbalanced load. Those need mechanical fixes.

### Why it matters specifically at 500mm

Your image scale is 1.81"/px. A star stays "round" for roughly 1.5–2 px of drift, so your error budget is about **3 arcseconds** for the whole exposure. A belt-driven GTi likely has periodic error in the ±15–25" range peak-to-peak — an order of magnitude over budget for a multi-minute sub. So:

- **Unguided at 500mm**: realistically 30–60 s subs, and you throw away maybe 20–40% of them.
- **Guided at 500mm**: 180–300 s subs, high keep rate.

Both can produce a good M31. Unguided just means many more frames and more total integration to reach the same signal-to-noise, since each sub[^guide-camera] pays the read-noise tax again. With the R5's fairly low read noise, this penalty is real but not crippling — **you can absolutely do step 5 unguided first**, then add guiding and immediately see the difference. I'd probably recommend that, since it isolates the variable.

### Shopping list if/when you add it

- ZWO **ASI120MM Mini** or **ASI220MM Mini** (~$150–250)
- **30mm f/4 or 32mm mini guide scope** (~$100–150)

- A mounting solution — hot-shoe bracket is the cheap path, but it's the least rigid; a side-by-side or dual-saddle plate is better and doesn't torque your camera's shoe.

Important constraint: **ASIAIR only accepts ZWO guide cameras.** Certainly true — this is ZWO's deliberate lock-in. Don't buy a QHY or Player One guide cam expecting it to enumerate.

Weight impact is ~500–700 g on top of your ~2.6 kg R5 + 100–500. The GTi's rated photographic payload is around 5 kg, so you're fine, but rebalance after adding it.

### The non-obvious part — this is also your R5 insurance policy

Recall the open risk that ASIAIR may not control the R5 at all. A guide camera changes the shape of that failure:

- ASIAIR can run **polar alignment and plate-solving off the guide camera alone**. It doesn't need the main camera for those.
- So the degraded mode becomes: ASIAIR handles polar align + goto + guiding via the guide cam, and you trigger the R5 independently with an intervalometer or the Canon app.

That's a perfectly workable rig. It means the guide camera isn't just a step-5 upgrade — it's the component that makes your whole ASIAIR purchase useful even in the worst case. Worth weighing when you decide timing.

### Where this lands in your plan

Steps 1–3 need **zero** guiding — polar align, moon, wide-field. Buy the guide setup when you commit to step 5, or earlier if the R5 compatibility test tonight comes back negative.

[^guide-camera]: A small secondary camera (mounted on a short guide scope alongside the main imaging camera) that repeatedly images a single star every 1–3 seconds to measure how far the mount has drifted off target. Its frames are never part of the final picture; they feed a closed loop that sends correction commands to the mount, cancelling tracking errors like RA periodic error and polar-alignment residual so longer exposures stay sharp. On an ASIAIR it can also serve as the sensor for polar alignment and plate-solving independently of the main camera.
