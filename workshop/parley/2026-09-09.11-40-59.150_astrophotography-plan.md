### Polar alignment and the ASIAIR decision

Three operations are worth separating:

| Operation | What it accomplishes |
|---|---|
| **Polar alignment** | Physically aligns the mount’s rotation axis with Earth’s axis. |
| **GoTo star alignment** | Establishes where the mount is pointing so it can locate targets. |
| **Guiding** | Measures a star’s movement and sends tracking corrections during imaging. |

GoTo alignment alone does not replace physical polar alignment; guiding is another layer, not the first step. ([inter-static.skywatcher.com](https://inter-static.skywatcher.com/downloads/synscan_app_manual_en_20230525.pdf?utm_source=openai))

**An ASIAIR is a sensible convenience upgrade, but “gold standard” is stronger than I would say.** ZWO confirms R5 support, and its documentation describes *all-sky polar alignment*, which does not require pointing near Polaris. That documentation labels the feature experimental, so I would not promise frictionless operation under every obstruction pattern. ([bbs.zwoastro.com](https://bbs.zwoastro.com/d/13144-canon-eos-r5?utm_source=openai))

The appealing part is that alignment can use your **main imaging camera**; buying a guide scope and camera is not a prerequisite for that workflow. You still physically turn the mount’s adjustment knobs—the software measures the error and guides your adjustments. See the [ASIAIR manual](https://i.zwoastro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/eee6be756814a97f867e7d136d98d810.pdf). ([i.zwoastro.com](https://i.zwoastro.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/eee6be756814a97f867e7d136d98d810.pdf?utm_source=openai))

My proposed buying sequence: **one existing-gear session → identify the main frustration → evaluate an ASIAIR → add guiding only when tracking is the demonstrated limitation.** I would compare available ASIAIR models rather than commit specifically to the Pro now.

| Setting | Starting point |

#### 3. Set up and roughly orient the mount

Level the tripod, orient the mount toward **true north**, and set its latitude adjustment to approximately **37.5°** for Menlo Park. Start from the GTi’s prescribed home position: counterweight down and camera pointing toward the north celestial pole. A phone compass is only a rough starting aid, not the final alignment measurement. Follow the [GTi setup manual](https://inter-static.skywatcher.com/downloads/sagti_manual_rev8c.pdf). #### 4. Choose one alignment route

**If Polaris is visible:** use SynScan Pro’s **Utility → Advanced → Polar Scope** display. Set the reticle orientation as instructed and place Polaris at the displayed position using the mount’s altitude/azimuth adjusters—**not simply at the crosshair center**. Reduce reticle illumination if it overwhelms the star. Then perform GoTo star alignment. ([inter-static.skywatcher.com](https://inter-static.skywatcher.com/downloads/synscan_app_manual_en_20230525.pdf?utm_source=openai))

**If Polaris is blocked:** use the existing software route:

1. Focus the camera on a bright star.
2. Perform **two-star alignment**, centering each star in the camera image.
3. Choose stars with **similar declinations**, as the SynScan manual specifies.
4. Open **Utility → Advanced → Polar Alignment**.
5. Follow the prompts carefully, using motor controls or physical altitude/azimuth adjustments only when requested.
6. Redo GoTo alignment after physically changing the mount’s orientation.

Importantly, the app manual specifies **exactly two stars**, and does **not** recommend three-star alignment for this polar-alignment routine. [SynScan’s instructions](https://inter-static.skywatcher.com/downloads/synscan_app_manual_en_20230525.pdf) are the relevant reference—not instructions for the separate hand controller. ([inter-static.skywatcher.com](https://inter-static.skywatcher.com/downloads/synscan_app_manual_en_20230525.pdf?utm_source=openai))

**My time limit would be 30 minutes for this first attempt.** If it stalls, record the exact step rather than repeatedly restarting without knowing what changed.

#### 5. Find M31 and test—not yet a long sequence

Use SynScan’s GoTo for **M31**, then take a test photograph to check its position. Ensure **sidereal tracking** is running. The GTi supports both GoTo and astronomical tracking. ([skywatcherusa.com](https://www.skywatcherusa.com/products/star-adventurer-gti-mount-kit?utm_source=openai))

My proposed test:

1. Take five 15-second exposures.
2. Inspect stars near the **center** at high magnification.
3. If they are compact, take five 30-second exposures.
4. Choose the longer exposure only if its stars remain acceptably round.
5. If both trail, try 100mm and shorter exposures before abandoning the session.

Do not diagnose every elongated star as polar-alignment error from a single frame. For now, record what changes with exposure length.

#### 6. Collect a repeatable sequence

My target would be **120 × 30 seconds**, if the tests pass: one hour of actual exposure.

Set the interval timer to approximately **35 seconds start-to-start**, and verify that the first few frames are being recorded. If using 15-second exposures, try a 20-second interval. These are proposed settings; the R5 manual documents the interval-timer controls. ([cam.start.canon](https://cam.start.canon/en/C003/manual/html/?utm_source=openai))

Inspect the first few frames, then periodically check focus, framing, lens moisture, and cable clearance. Do not change zoom or focus casually during the sequence.

#### 7. Before changing the optical setup: capture flats

If practical, take **20–30 evenly illuminated flat frames**, keeping the same zoom, focus, aperture, and ISO. Adjust shutter speed so the illumination is comfortably below clipping. Flats help correct uneven illumination and dust shadows; their calibration matters, so retain the originals and we can choose a complete processing workflow afterward. ([siril.org](https://siril.org/tutorials/tuto-manual/))

For tonight, I would prioritize good target exposures and flats over attempting every calibration-frame type.
