## Miami Cargo Plane Crash — Details

**What happened:** The aircraft, operated by 21 Air, was contracted to carry cargo for Amazon, with the flight originally taking off from Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport in San Juan, Puerto Rico. The FAA said a Boeing 767-300 overran the runway at 2pm ET (18:00 GMT) on Sunday, September 6, upon arriving from San Juan.

**Casualties:** Five people died and three of five injured remain hospitalized (two critical, one stable). The deceased were identified as Rolando Aleman Leon, 55; Yoel Rodriguez Narajo, 53; Julio C. Pineda, 75; Carlos Acosta Fajardo, 53; and Javierkys Reyes Quevedo, 47. All five killed were in a van owned by Professional Ocean Service Corp., a company that cleans planes.

**Cause (still under investigation):** Flight data suggests the cargo plane tried to abort an unsteady landing before smashing into the van, according to information investigators released Tuesday. Pilots reportedly applied full power seconds before the plane overran the runway. NTSB investigators said they were looking into all factors, from potential mechanical failure to environmental variables or flight crew performance — there were thunderstorms and winds in the area, but it's too early to say if weather played a role.

**Safety systems debate:** Some 70 U.S. airports have runway "arrestor bed" (EMAS) stopping systems, but the FAA doesn't require them if there's a 1,000-foot runway safety area, which Miami has; the FAA credits EMAS with slowing/stopping flights carrying nearly 500 crew and passengers elsewhere. This has raised questions about whether such a system could have prevented the deaths.

**Airport impact:** As of Monday evening, two of the four runways remained closed, and officials said they wouldn't reopen until the investigation concluded.

Investigation is ongoing (NTSB, as of Sept. 8-9 reports); no final cause has been determined.
